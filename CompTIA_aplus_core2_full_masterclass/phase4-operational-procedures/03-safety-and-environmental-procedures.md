# 03 — Safety Procedures & Environmental Controls

## Electrical safety

| Hazard | Prevention |
|---|---|
| **Electrostatic Discharge (ESD)** | Anti-static wrist strap, anti-static mats, keep components in anti-static bags until installed — even a small unfelt static discharge can damage sensitive components |
| **Electrical fire risk** | Never use water on an electrical fire — use a **Class C** fire extinguisher (or Class ABC multi-purpose) |
| **Power supply hazards** | PSUs store charge even when unplugged — don't open them; treat as a factory-sealed unit for repair purposes |

## Fire extinguisher classes (memorize)

| Class | For |
|---|---|
| **A** | Ordinary combustibles (wood, paper, cloth) |
| **B** | Flammable liquids |
| **C** | Electrical equipment |
| **D** | Combustible metals |
| **K** | Kitchen/cooking oils |

## Physical safety

- **Proper lifting technique**: lift with legs, not the back, to avoid injury when moving heavy equipment (servers, monitors, UPS units)
- **Cable management**: prevent trip hazards, avoid running cables across walkways
- **Weight limitations**: know equipment rack/lift weight limits before moving

## Environmental controls for equipment/server rooms

| Control | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Temperature/humidity monitoring** | Overheating damages hardware; excess humidity risks corrosion/short circuits; too dry increases static risk |
| **Air filters/dust prevention** | Dust buildup restricts airflow, causes overheating |
| **Proper ventilation/airflow** | Hot/cold aisle arrangement in server rooms for efficient cooling |

## Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS) / Safety Data Sheets (SDS)

Documents that detail:
- Hazardous material handling procedures
- Proper disposal methods
- Emergency response procedures (spills, exposure)

**Exam trap:** "A toner cartridge spills — what document tells you how to safely clean it up?" → Consult the SDS (Safety Data Sheet) for that specific product.

## Regulatory compliance for disposal

- **Batteries, toner/ink, CRT monitors, and other e-waste** typically have specific local/regional disposal regulations — never just throw them in regular trash
- Follow local government/environmental regulations for disposal of hazardous materials

## Quick self-check
1. What class of fire extinguisher is correct for an electrical fire?
2. What tool/practice prevents ESD damage when handling internal components?
3. What document should you consult for proper handling/disposal of a specific hazardous material?
4. Why is humidity monitoring important in a server room, both too high and too low?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Class C (or a multi-purpose Class ABC extinguisher).
2. Anti-static wrist strap and anti-static mat.
3. The Safety Data Sheet (SDS), formerly called MSDS.
4. Too high humidity risks corrosion/short circuits; too low humidity increases static electricity risk (ESD).
</details>
